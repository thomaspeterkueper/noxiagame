# Q1 social fabric and station intelligence

Q1 does not assume the nuclear family as its default social unit.

## Residential and relationship structure
Residents may live in small private units, couples, multi-partner relationships, friendship households and WG-like residential collectives. A residential collective is not automatically a romantic or sexual relationship. Person-to-person relationships remain explicit and independent, so one group can contain partners, friends, siblings, co-parents and unrelated residents without collapsing them into one relationship type.

People may belong to several groups simultaneously: residential collective, care circle, learning cohort, club and project group.

## Children and care
A child's care network can include biological parents, social parents, partners of parents, relatives, trusted members of the residential collective and professional carers. Care responsibility is therefore a group membership property in addition to individual relationships.

Shared care must not mean diffuse responsibility. At any simulated time, safeguarding and practical supervision must resolve to identifiable responsible adults/services. The Learning Commons complements the care network rather than replacing it.

## Q1 Station Intelligence
The station intelligence is a persistent non-human institutional agent, not a Person and not an omniscient narrator.

Its permitted domains include life-support coordination, transport/scheduling, public information, learning support, care coordination, maintenance triage and emergencies. It may influence daily life through recommendations, resource allocation within policy, reminders, routing and escalation.

It has bounded sensors/data access and explicit policy. It must not automatically know private conversations, memories or motives. Interventions should be attributable and explainable. Human residents can disagree with recommendations; policy and emergency authority determine when the AI may override a preference.

This makes the AI part of Q1 society: residents can trust it, dislike it, depend on it, challenge it or campaign to change its policies without turning it into hidden game-master logic.
