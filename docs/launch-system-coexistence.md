# Launch-system coexistence in NOXIA

NOXIA treats launch technologies as coexisting infrastructures, not as a linear unlock chain where a newer system automatically deletes an older one.

## Shared Core boundary

Both supported architecture classes converge on the same lifecycle:

- `vehicle-native-ascent`
- `carrier-rocket`

After launch-system readiness is satisfied, both hand off to the existing Ascent Core, then to orbital insertion, orbital arrival, Arrival Control and Docking. No architecture receives its own duplicate orbit or transit model.

## Why legacy launchers remain relevant

A carrier rocket can remain rational after reusable or spacecraft-native systems exist because different launch systems can occupy different economic niches. NOXIA should be able to express these differences through authority-backed values rather than a global technology ranking.

Relevant dimensions include:

- payload mass and volume envelope
- crew certification
- launch-site availability
- pad/tower/fueling compatibility
- discarded hardware per flight
- reusable hardware and refurbishment burden
- consumables and propellant
- turnaround time and achievable cadence
- payload integration effort
- recovery operations
- insurance/risk exposure
- service ownership versus player-owned infrastructure

Examples of resulting gameplay choices include buying a launch service instead of owning launch hardware, keeping an older expendable family for rare heavy payloads, using reusable systems for high-cadence logistics, or maintaining multiple pad classes because fleet generations overlap.

## Authority rule

NOXIA may model the economic consequences, but it must not invent launch performance, stage masses, propellant quantities, refurbishment intervals or payload envelopes. Those values come from KUEPER Engineering. Prices and market behavior remain NOXIA economy concerns.
